    Using this when you make a decision

    1. Start with the market mood. When volatility is elevated, the same position swings further in both directions. If you would not be comfortable with the bad-day figure in the calculator, the position is larger than your comfort, whatever you think of the company.
    2. Check the company's own news. Look for its ticker under Filings, Filing language and Insider activity in the brief below. Every filing links to the SEC original; read it rather than the label.
    3. Compare with an ordinary day. Each event type's history is shown beside "any ordinary day" in the same universe. If they are about the same, the event on its own tells you very little.
    4. Know how sure a pattern is. NOT MET means a pre-registered test looked and found no reliable edge. Never tested means a pattern that could be chance. Neither is a reason to act by itself.
    5. Decide what would prove you wrong, before you act. Write it down as a thesis with a falsifier; the bots track every open thesis and flag new filings that bear on it.
    6. Declare what you hold. Until positions are declared, the bots can't measure your concentration or your dollar risk — the two numbers most likely to matter.

    Investing basics, in two minutes

    General principles most beginners are never told. They are education, not advice about any particular investment.

    Risk and reward travel together

    Anything that can rise a lot can fall a lot. Small companies and Bitcoin swing far more than the S&P 500; the "bad day" figures on this page show how much more.

    Time is your cushion

    Money you need within a few years is exposed to whatever the market does in those years. Stocks have tended to recover from falls, but some falls took years, and Bitcoin has dropped 75–85% after each past peak.

    Don't put everything in one place

    One company can fail outright. A fund holding hundreds of companies (like SPY) spreads that risk. The bots' Exposure section measures how concentrated you are, once you declare holdings.

    Nobody reliably times the market

    This project tested several ideas for beating the market with strict rules, and every one failed. Be wary of anyone, or any chart, that claims to know what happens next.

    Size before story

    Before buying, decide how much you could lose without it changing your life, and size the position to that. The calculator above turns tonight's risk figures into dollars.

    Write down why, and what would change your mind

    Before you act, write one sentence on why, and one on what would prove you wrong. It is the simplest protection against panic selling and against holding a loser out of hope.

    Glossary

    Every term the brief uses, in plain words.

    Ticker
    The short code a stock trades under (SPY, IWM, AAPL).
    SPY · IWM
    Funds that track the S&P 500 (500 large US companies) and the Russell 2000 (2,000 small ones). Used as the yardsticks: "vs SPY" means "compared with simply owning the S&P 500".
    Watched universe
    The ~1,000 small US companies the bots follow: common stock on a main exchange, price at least $2, and traded actively enough to buy and sell. It is a list of what is watched, not a list of what to buy.
    Trading day ("bar")
    One day the market is open. 21 trading days is about a month; 63 is about three months.
    8-K
    A form a company must file within four business days of a major event. Each "item" number is a type of event, for example 3.01 = a stock exchange warning that the company is breaking a listing rule, 5.02 = an executive or director leaving or joining, 1.01 = a significant new contract or loan.
    10-K · 10-Q
    The annual and quarterly reports. LIBRARIAN compares each one with the company's previous one and reports risk phrases that appeared or disappeared ("going concern", "delisting", "covenant").
    Form 4
    Filed when a company insider (an executive, director, or 10% owner) trades the company's shares. Code P = bought on the open market with their own money; S = sold. Grants, option exercises and tax withholding are not purchases and are labelled "other".
    Cluster buy
    Two or more different insiders buying on the open market within five days, at least $100,000 in total.
    Base rate
    What happened, historically, after events of one type: how the stock did over the next month or three months compared with SPY. It describes the past. It does not say what this company will do.
    Median
    The middle outcome: half did better, half did worse. Used instead of the average because a few huge winners can make an average look good when most cases lost.
    Middle half
    The range covering the middle 50% of past outcomes: a quarter did worse than the low end, a quarter better than the high end.
    % positive
    The share of past cases where the stock beat SPY over that period.
    Any ordinary day
    The comparison line: what happened from an arbitrary date in the same universe, measured the same way. Small companies lagged the S&P 500 in 2023–26, so most event types look negative; what matters is the difference from this line.
    NOT MET
    A pre-registered test was run on this pattern, with its pass mark fixed in advance, and the pattern failed: it could not be told apart from luck.
    Described, never tested
    A pattern that has only been described, not tested. It may be chance. These event types were deliberately given up so they can be shown, and can never be tested later.
    Volatility (%/yr)
    How much a price typically swings, scaled to a year. 20% is ordinary for the S&P 500; 30%+ means noticeably bigger daily moves than usual. It says how far, not which way.
    1-day 99% loss bound
    On about 1 trading day in 100, the loss has been worse than this. Not the worst case: on those days, it can be much worse.
    1-month 90% band
    The range that about 9 months in 10 would end inside, if conditions stay as they are. Not a target, not a forecast of direction.
    GJR-GARCH · EWMA
    The two methods used to estimate volatility. GJR-GARCH is used for stocks with long, active trading histories; EWMA (a weighted average of recent swings) everywhere else. Each was checked against years of data it had not seen.
    VIX
    The market's own estimate of S&P 500 volatility over the next month, from option prices. Under 15 calm, 15–20 normal, 20–30 elevated, over 30 stressed.
    High-yield credit spread
    The extra interest riskier companies pay to borrow, over US Treasuries. Rising means lenders are getting nervous, which hits small, indebted companies first.
    10-year · 2-year yield, curve
    Interest rates on US government debt. The "10y minus 2y" spread near or below zero has often come before slowdowns.
    Fed funds rate
    The Federal Reserve's policy rate: the base cost of money.
    Jobless claims · unemployment · CPI
    Weekly new unemployment claims, the monthly unemployment rate, and the consumer price index (inflation). Context for the economy, not for a single stock.
    Thesis · falsifier
    Your written reason for owning something, and the specific thing that would prove it wrong. The bots track both and show filings that bear on them.
    Stance
    Conservative, balanced or aggressive. It changes the order of the brief and which concentration levels get flagged. It never changes a number.
    Bitcoin halving
    Every 210,000 blocks (about four years) the number of new bitcoins created per block is cut in half. Past halvings: Nov 2012, Jul 2016, May 2020, Apr 2024. The next is estimated for around April 2028.
    Four-year cycle
    The observation that Bitcoin's big booms (about 17–18 months after a halving) and busts (about 30 months after) have come at similar times in past cycles. A pattern, not a law.
    Drawdown ("% below the high")
    How far a price is under its highest point. A 50% drawdown needs a 100% gain to get back to the high.
    Inflation (CPI)
    How much the prices of everyday goods rose over a year. The Federal Reserve aims for about 2%.
    Recession
    A broad, lasting fall in economic activity, usually with rising unemployment. Stock prices often fall before and during one.
    Dark
    A section whose data is missing or stale. It is shown as dark rather than showing old numbers as if they were new.

    The programmes

    Each carries a K budget: a fixed number of reads. Declaring a specification costs nothing; reading one spends a slot permanently, and anti-deferral means it can never be re-read — not even if a bug turns up afterwards. The budget is what stops a search from running until it finds a number it likes.

    The reads

    Every criterion below was written into the registry before the data existed. Nothing was chosen after seeing a result, and each specification records its own prior — the probability the operator signed, in advance, that it would pass.

    What the record actually shows

    The same failure appears in three separate reads, across two programmes and two unrelated mechanisms: the effect is absent from the first third of the sample.

    Stability asks whether an edge runs through the whole window or sits concentrated in part of it. form4_cluster_buy_v2, value_ebit_ev_v1 and value_ebit_ev_v2 each returned a non-positive first third. An effect that is not there for part of the sample is not one to rely on for the next part — and that is more durable than any individual verdict.

    The v25 pipeline that came before these — price-derived features, 280 large caps, a five-day horizon, daily rebalance — returned a walk-forward AUC of 0.4942 over roughly 160,000 observations, zero of 305 models with a positive deflated Sharpe, and White Reality Check and Hansen SPA p-values of 0.486 and 0.948. It was retired on 2026-09-29 and its code deleted on 2026-11-08.

    What survives

    The engineering, which was never the part that failed. A point-in-time universe and fundamentals panel built without look-ahead — the half most people get wrong and never find out. qt/, twelve tested modules. A Referee that counts reads and refuses a second one. And the pre-registration discipline that makes a negative answer worth trusting.
